Three Births of a Lawgiver: Lycurgus and Sparta一个立法者的三次诞生——吕库古和斯巴达

烟霞第 167 期 · 2026 年Yanxia No. 167 · 2026

普鲁塔克在《吕库古传》开篇就告诉读者：关于这位立法者，没有任何一件事情是不引起争议的——家世、出行、死亡、立法的方式，各家说法都不一样；至于他生活的年代，分歧更大。然后他写下四万字，成为此后两千年里全欧洲关于斯巴达制度最主要的知识来源。 - 公元前七世纪的斯巴达诗人歌颂本邦秩序，唯独没有提到那位创制者的名字；到色诺芬笔下，每一条规矩才都署上了吕库古。一项制度先有制度、后有作者——这中间发生了什么？ - 当一份改革纲领被追认成四百年前的古制，我们今天读到的究竟是记录，还是倒影？ - 斯巴达全权公民从普拉提亚的五千人降到留克特拉的约七百人。人心不古，还是财产结构的必然？ - 从柏拉图到卢梭，同一个斯巴达被读成了完全不同的药方。一个没有可靠事迹的立法者，为什么反而是最好用的？

At the very beginning of his Life of Lycurgus, Plutarch tells the reader that nothing can be said about this lawgiver that is not disputed: his family, his travels, his death and the way he made his laws are all told differently by different sources, and on the period in which he lived there is even greater disagreement. He then went on to write some forty thousand words, which became Europe's main source of knowledge about Spartan institutions for the next two thousand years. - Spartan poets of the seventh century BC praised their city's order but never named its founder; only in Xenophon does every rule come signed with Lycurgus's name. The institutions came first and the author afterwards: what happened in between? - When a reform programme is retroactively recast as an ancient constitution four hundred years old, is what we read today a record, or a reflection? - The number of full Spartan citizens fell from five thousand at Plataea to about seven hundred at Leuctra. Was this moral decline, or the inevitable result of how property was held? - From Plato to Rousseau, the same Sparta was read as a prescription for entirely different cures. Why is a lawgiver with no reliable history the most useful kind?
